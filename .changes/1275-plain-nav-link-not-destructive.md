### Fixed

- **`@reticlehq/browser` — a plain navigation link is no longer refused as a destructive action.** The guard reads a control's `href` along with its text, which is right for a button whose label is an icon: its `formAction` is the only place it says what it does. On an anchor the href is an ADDRESS, and addresses carry the pattern's words, so `Orders & invoices` pointing at `/billing/payment` was blocked for the `payment` in the URL, and the way past a block is `confirmDangerous: true` passed reflexively. An `<a href>` with no handler and no form around it, pointing at a scheme that fetches a document, is a GET that changes nothing, so it is now classified on the act it performs rather than on its address. The exemption is narrow and it is only granted on a POSITIVE reading of the element: only a real anchor counts (a `role="link"` alone does not), the href must be http(s) or relative (`javascript:` and `data:` are refused, and the href's origin is NOT compared against the page's), the click-handler question is three-valued — a link is exempted only when a probe actually read the element and found no handler, never when nothing could inspect it, which is the shape a handler bound with plain `addEventListener` has — a framework probe answers "no handler" only after walking the element's ancestors, since a delegated `onClick` on a parent runs on a bubbling click just the same, and an anchor carrying a marker that a framework rewrites into a non-GET request (`data-method`, `data-turbo-method`, `data-remote`, `hx-delete` and friends) keeps its block. A link with a handler, a link inside a form, every button, and a drag end all keep their block. Closes [#1275](https://github.com/reticlehq/reticle/issues/1275).
